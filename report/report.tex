\section{Problem}

Mobile money runs on trust. In Bangladesh, wallets like upay move everyday
money for millions of people, and one scam call or one hijacked account can
wipe out a user's savings along with their faith in digital payments. Fraud
analysts fight this daily, but they drown in alerts. A typical system shows a
bare risk score with no explanation, so every serious case costs a long manual
investigation: pulling timelines, checking devices and locations, and hunting
for connected wallets. Slow reviews mean stolen money keeps moving, analysts
burn out, and users leave the platform. Our project tackles exactly this gap
for upay: fast scoring is not enough, analysts need to understand each case
and act on it with confidence.

\section{Proposed idea}

We proposed Vigil, an AI investigation console that answers the three
questions every fraud case raises. What happened? Why is it risky? What should
upay do next? Instead of another dashboard of unexplained scores, Vigil scores
each transfer, explains the score in plain auditable reasons, maps it to a
clear action, and writes the whole case up as a short narrative in English or
Bangla. High risk never blocks money on its own. It holds the transaction,
asks for step up authentication, and puts a human analyst in charge.

\section{Implemented solution}

The solution has three parts that work as one loop. First, a real time scoring
API built with FastAPI. A transfer arrives as sender, receiver, amount,
device, location, and timestamp, and the API returns a risk score between 0
and 1, a level of Low, Medium, or High, the top three reasons, a recommended
action, and a written narrative. Second, an analyst console, a static web
page with no build step, showing a risk sorted queue with level filters and
search, a case detail view with the narrative, reasons, component scores, a
mule ring panel, and a wallet timeline, plus a scoring playground and decision
buttons that log Allow, Step up, or Freeze for future retraining. Third, an
offline evaluation harness that measures the system on held out data and
reports ranking quality, fairness across districts and account ages, latency,
and business impact. All development used synthetic data we generated
ourselves plus public data, following privacy by design. No production
customer data was needed or used.

\section{Key features}

Vigil scores transfers in about 25 milliseconds at the 95th percentile.
Every score carries human readable reasons, with per transaction SHAP values
backing them up. The console supports English and Bangla narratives, with a
deterministic offline fallback so the demo works without internet. The
transaction graph panel exposes mule rings by listing fraud wallets within
two hops of a receiver. Analyst decisions feed a retraining log, and
thresholds live in a config file so new on site requirements can be integrated
without touching the models. Inputs are strictly validated, unauthenticated
scoring traffic is isolated so it can never poison anyone's features, and the
test suite covers scoring, validation, security regressions, and the language
model path.

\section{AI approach}

Our data strategy mixes our own hardened simulator, with scam, takeover,
mule, and agent patterns plus realistic noise, and PaySim, a public mobile
money dataset calibrated on real logs, used only for independent validation.
The model layer combines four techniques. An XGBoost classifier learns fraud
shapes from tens of thousands of labeled examples. An IsolationForest learns
what normal looks like from legitimate traffic alone and flags the rest, with
scores calibrated to percentiles from training data. A NetworkX graph adds
suspicion for receivers sitting near known fraud wallets. A weighted ensemble
of 0.7, 0.2, and 0.1 blends the three into one score. A grounded language
model then narrates each case from the computed facts only, under a fixed
template, with sanitized inputs. On our test data the system reaches 0.998
AUC against 0.81 for hand written rules, and on PaySim it holds 0.90 while
the rules collapse to a coin flip, using the same code with no tuning. We
deliberately refused leakage features that would have printed perfect scores
and proved nothing.

\section{Intended real life impact}

For analysts, Vigil turns a fifteen minute manual investigation into a two
minute review, which our business simulation converts into loss prevented in
BDT and analyst hours saved per thousand high risk holds. For users, faster
holds on scams and takeovers mean stolen money stops moving sooner, and clear
warnings protect first time and vulnerable customers before they pay a
stranger. For upay, every held scam is trust kept, and trust is what grows a
wallet. The API contract is stateless and ready to plug into a real stream,
with weekly retraining on governed labels as the path from this prototype to a
pilot. Responsible by design throughout: synthetic data only, explainable
scores, fairness measured, human oversight on every consequential action.
